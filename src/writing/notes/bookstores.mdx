Like many people, I’m charmed by bookstores. I’m planting this note to collect quotes and observations about them.

---

Robin Sloan, in his Aspire Zine, _Information technology should aspire to the speed and privacy of the printed page_:

> A person visits a bookstore not primarily because they need a book, but because they are the kind of person who visits a bookstore.

(The zine is a ~1,500 word creative essay & experiment on how e-books should work. It’s sold out, but [email me](mailto:pete@petemillspaugh.com) if you’d like to borrow my “copy”—I own the printed version, and I can lend you my e-copy.)

Robin again, in his October 2026 newsletter [_Peaking over the wall_](https://www.robinsloan.com/newsletters/peeking-over-the-wall/):

> I mentioned that Kevin’s launch was at Kepler’s, and folks, let me just report—remind you—reassure you: nothing, and I do mean nothing, beats a big, bright bookstore full of people.

---

James Daunt, quoted in Nathan Heller’s _New Yorker_ profile “[The New Age of Reading](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/10/26/james-daunt-profile)”:

> “Books aren’t a fixed market. The more bookshops there are, the more people will read. The better bookshops are, the more people discover things. Online is an efficient place, but it’s not a place that can excite in itself.”

The whole piece is worth reading if you, too, are the kind of person who visits a bookstore. It’s part profile of Daunt and part recent history (and myth-busting) about the bookstore industry.
